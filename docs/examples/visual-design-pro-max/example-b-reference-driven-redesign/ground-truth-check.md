# Ground-truth check — image observations vs the real CSS

**Why this file exists.** The reference image in this example is a render of a file this repository owns (`preview/xiaomaomi-app.html`), so for once the true answers are known. Every observation in `inputs/reference-analysis.json` (made by the Principal agent after viewing `inputs/xiaomaomi-390x844.png` with the model's image reader) is compared below against the CSS and classified **confirmed / partially confirmed / wrong**. The point is to test whether the confidence grading (`observed / high / assumption / not-derivable`) separates what a screenshot can prove from what it cannot. Hand-written; CSS line numbers refer to `preview/xiaomaomi-app.html` (411 lines).

## Pixel share: eye estimate vs `extract-colors`

| Source | Palette (hex · share · role guess) |
|---|---|
| `palette_observed` in `reference-analysis.json` (model's eye estimate) | `#FFF7ED` 42 % background · `#FFFFFF` 28 % surface · `#F97316` 6 % primary · `#FED7AA` 5 % outline · `#9A3412` 5 % text · `#BFDBFE` 2 % secondary · `#A7F3D0` 2 % secondary |
| `inputs/palette-extracted.json` (`reference_analysis.py extract-colors`, median cut on a 76 × 200 downsample, k = 10, 3 kept, confidence `assumption`) | `#FFFFFF` 71.1 % background · `#FEF5E9` 20.7 % surface · `#EACCB4` 8.2 % unknown |

Discrepancies and what they mean:

1. **Area is inverted.** The eye put cream first (42 %) and white second (28 %); the pixels say white 71 %, cream 21 %. Cards cover most of the frame; the cream is only gutters and gaps. Eye estimates are good at *roles* and bad at *area* — the role guesses in the eye list are all right, the shares are not.
2. **Small saturated regions vanish.** Orange (6 % by eye), peach borders, brown text and the blue/green tiles do not survive a k = 10 median cut at 76 × 200: they are folded into `#EACCB4` (8.2 %), a mixture colour that **does not exist in the CSS**. A pixel-share palette must never be promoted to a seed on its own; it is a cross-check for the big surfaces.
3. **Validation caught the near-duplicate, not the inversion.** `reference_analysis.py validate` warned `R-PAL-3: #FFF7ED and #FFFFFF are near-duplicates (distance 19.7)` — a useful nudge, but nothing in the tooling compares the two lists. The analysis kept the eye estimate in `palette_observed`; `extract-colors` output is attached as evidence only. The right practice: keep the eye list for roles, replace its `share` values with the extractor's where a colour appears in both, and mark the rest `assumption`.

## Take-aways for the redesign (what the art director does with this)

1. Trust the `observed` rows as the brief: cream page, white cards, one orange, brown-orange text, peach 2 px outlines, 20–28 card radius, 4-item nav, dot-list rows. The contract now carries all of these.
2. Answer the `unknowns` at the **profile** level, not per page: confirm `Fredoka` / `Nunito` (the classifications already point at the right kinds of face), set `rendering.shadow` to a double/clay set, set `motion.duration_scale.base` to 200, confirm or replace the placeholder accent `#B74B00`, confirm the default gold reward `#F5A524` and the darkened success green `#107435` (both listed in `unknowns`), and decide whether `secondary` should be a brand-level seed or a category tint. Re-run `vd.py init --force` and `vd.py validate`.
3. Demote `style.confidence` to `assumption` until a second reference or the user confirms the small-control details (icon-button shape, nav plate shape, inactive nav colour).
4. Keep the `not-derivable` list as the question list for the user; it was right every time.
